# Practicalize FDE Delivery

Make the customer able to perform the work without undocumented author knowledge.

## Workflow

1. Read [references/customer-adoption-contract.md](references/customer-adoption-contract.md).
2. Choose the customer's current and target maturity level; do not assume every customer should reach maximum autonomy.
3. Define the business baseline, accountable sponsor, workflow owner, user population, stop condition, and measurement source.
4. Create discovery questions, current-state workflow, use-case qualification, autonomy tier, architecture options, and an ADR backlog.
5. Define a thin vertical slice using real boundary shapes with approved sandbox data and systems.
6. Add security, evaluation, reliability, cost, change, support, rollback, and manual-fallback gates.
7. Add production rollout cohorts and adoption telemetry, not just technical deployment.
8. Add customer pair-delivery, teach-back, incident simulation, and competency evidence.
9. Capture sanitized repeatable patterns and product-friction evidence using
   [assets/google-field-feedback.md](assets/google-field-feedback.md); never copy
   customer-confidential data into the handbook or an external feedback system.
10. Define scale, reuse, model/platform evolution, and retirement triggers.
11. Use [assets/engagement-charter.md](assets/engagement-charter.md) as the minimum engagement record.

## Delivery rule

The FDE owns clarity and hands-on progress, but the customer owns business decisions, risk acceptance, production authorization, and the eventual service. Success is measured by workflow impact, safe production usage, and customer independence—not demo completion.
