## Key evidence findings

1. Agent Platform locations are capability-specific and volatile. Runtime, Sessions, Memory Bank, and Gateway cannot be assumed to have identical availability.
2. Current Agent Gateway setup guidance requires the Gemini Enterprise app, Runtime agents, Gateway, and associated Registry in the same project. For Runtime, the regional Registry is in the same project and region as agents and Gateway.
3. Shared VPC separates central network ownership from service-project workload ownership; IAM and organization policies remain separate controls.
4. VPC Service Controls mitigates data-exfiltration risk for supported services and does not replace IAM. Perimeters require dependency analysis and dry-run rollout to avoid outages.
5. The official multi-tenant agentic AI architecture uses hub-and-spoke routing/governance and dedicated tenant projects with identity, PAB and perimeter controls.
6. The linked official Terraform is a reference implementation, not evidence that customer-specific production state, CI identity, recovery, logging, quota, IAM, residency and operational requirements are complete.
7. Cloud Foundation Fabric is an official modular landing-zone toolkit intended to be adopted deliberately; its release/provider constraints and module contracts must remain pinned and qualified.
8. Agent-local reasoning workflows and durable cross-service business orchestration have different failure, wait, replay and ownership semantics.

## Evidence boundaries

- Product names, stages, regions, quotas, APIs and topology constraints require revalidation immediately before customer design approval.
- Architecture Center material provides official reference guidance but does not decide customer regulatory, contractual, residency or operating requirements.
- Official source and samples demonstrate implementation patterns; they are not managed-service guarantees or customer-ready deployment approvals.
- Project isolation does not replace resource authorization, trusted tenant context, data partitioning, cache isolation, or negative testing.
- An approved deployment region variable does not prove all selected capabilities are available or contractually acceptable there.
